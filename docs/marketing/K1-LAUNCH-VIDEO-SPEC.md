# SpectraSynq K1 Launch Video — Complete Production Specification

## Overview

A 55-second cinematic reveal that introduces the SpectraSynq K1 to the world. This is not a product demonstration. It is the moment music becomes visible.

The video proves the K1's musical intelligence through progressive instrument isolation (drums, strings, voice), then fusion. The viewer moves through curiosity, recognition, fascination, and desire in under a minute.

**Psychological structure:**

1. **Cause-effect recognition** — show the sound source, then show the K1's response
2. **Progressive capability reveal** — rhythm, then harmony, then voice, then layered music
3. **Emotional climax** — the layered scene demonstrates full potential before the silence drop

**Runtime:** 55 seconds
**Aspect ratios:** 16:9 (primary), 9:16 (social), 1:1 (social)
**Delivery:** 4K master, 1080p web

## 3. AUDIO PRODUCTION SPEC

### Architecture: Stems + Master

The audio is composed as **separate stems** that layer together, enabling:
- Precise sync of individual instruments to K1 behaviour during filming
- Independent control of each instrument's level in the mix
- The ability to play stems individually during the isolation segments and layer them during fusion

### Recording Specification

| Element | Method | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Drums | Pre-recorded in studio. Close-mic on floor tom (dynamic mic, e.g. Sennheiser MD 421) + overhead for hi-hat. Minimal processing. | Must be recorded to a click at exactly 100 BPM so the K1 can be synchronised. |
| Cello | Pre-recorded in studio or sourced from a session musician. Close-mic (large diaphragm condenser, e.g. Neumann U87 or similar). | Record both the solo passage and the ensemble swell as separate takes to the same click. |
| Voice | Pre-recorded in studio. Close-mic (large diaphragm condenser). Minimal processing — the raw human quality is essential. | Record the solo riff and the ensemble vocal separately. |
| Drone | Synthesised in DAW (Serum, Vital, or analogue synth). | Very low frequency content. Mix at -20dB below the music bed. Subwoofer-only in cinema systems. |

### Sync Method

**Pre-recorded stems played back through a studio monitor positioned near the K1's microphone during filming.** The K1 responds in real time to the acoustic playback. This captures authentic K1 behaviour (not simulated or programmed).

Alternative for controlled takes: feed the audio directly into the K1's I2S input via a line-level-to-microphone converter, bypassing room acoustics for perfectly clean audio input.

**On-set audio is NOT the final audio.** The production audio (K1 responding to monitor playback) captures the visual performance. The final audio mix uses the original studio stems, synchronised in post.

## 4. BLENDER RENDER REQUIREMENTS

### Render vs. Real Breakdown

| Content | Source | Rationale |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| K1 device with active LEDs | **Real hardware** (all segments) | Authentic light behaviour cannot be simulated convincingly. The K1's LGP diffusion, centre-origin patterns, and audio-reactive timing are the product's core value proposition. Faking them undermines credibility. |
| Instrument close-ups (drum, cello, voice) | **Real footage** (or high-quality stock) | Human performance and tactile instrument detail cannot be matched in CG within budget. |
| End card wordmark (H5) | **Motion graphics** (After Effects or Blender) | Simple 2D text animation. |
| Environmental wide shot (G3) — potential | **Real set preferred**, Blender backup | If the filming location cannot deliver the desired "K1 lights the room" effect, a Blender composite is the fallback. |

### Blender Requirements (if needed for G3 environmental composite or any pickup shots)

#### K1 Model

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Geometry | Accurate K1 dimensions. Aluminium housing with brushed finish. Acrylic LGP with edge-lit diffusion simulation. |
| LED simulation | 160 emissive points per strip (320 total), individually addressable. Centre-origin at point 79/80. Emission shader with energy falloff matching real LGP diffusion characteristics. |
| Material | PBR aluminium (roughness ~0.3, anisotropic). Acrylic LGP (subsurface scattering for light guide behaviour, IOR 1.49). |
| Scale | 1:1 real-world scale. |

#### Scene Setup

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Environment | Simple room: dark walls (matte black or deep charcoal), reflective surface beneath K1 (polished concrete or dark wood table). |
| Lighting | **K1 LEDs are the only light source.** No HDRI, no area lights, no sun. The render must match the real-footage aesthetic where the K1 illuminates its own scene. |
| Camera | Match real camera parameters: 50mm equivalent, f/1.8, slight DOF. Motion blur at 180-degree shutter (1/48s at 24fps). |

#### LED Behaviour Animation

Each segment's K1 visual behaviour would be driven by:

1. **Keyframed emission values** on each of the 320 LED points, exported from the K1's actual serial capture data (use `capture stream` to record frame-by-frame LED state as RGB arrays)
2. **Python driver script** in Blender that reads the captured LED data and applies it to the emission shader intensity and colour of each point per frame

This ensures the Blender render matches the real K1 behaviour exactly, not an artist's interpretation.

#### Render Settings

| Requirement | Detail |

| Instrument | Option A: Live Talent | Option B: Stock Footage | Decision Criteria |

## 6. POST-PRODUCTION SPEC

### Edit Rhythm and Pacing

| Segment | Pacing | Cut Rhythm | Notes |
|---------|--------|------------|-------|
| A (0:00–0:03) | Very slow | 1 cut | Stillness. Patience. Let the viewer settle. |
| B (0:03–0:07) | Slow | 1 cut | The push-in creates movement without cutting. The cut to black is the only edit. |
| C (0:07–0:14) | Fast, precise | 5 cuts in 7 seconds | Percussive editing. Every cut lands on a transient. The editing IS the rhythm. |
| D (0:14–0:21) | Slow, flowing | 2 cuts in 7 seconds | The cello is legato; the editing is legato. Let shots breathe. |
| E (0:21–0:27) | Medium | 2 cuts in 6 seconds | Intimate pacing. The cut to K1 lingers. |
| F (0:27–0:35) | Medium, building | 2 cuts in 8 seconds | The orbit creates continuous movement. Cuts are dissolves, not hard. |
| G (0:35–0:43) | Fast, escalating | 4 cuts in 8 seconds | Quick cuts mirror the building music. Each angle is a new revelation. |
| H (0:43–0:55) | Very slow → still | 3 cuts in 12 seconds | The silence demands stillness. The viewer needs time to feel. |

**Total cuts:** ~20 across 55 seconds.
**Average shot length:** 2.75 seconds.
**Shortest shot:** G2 (1.5 seconds — LGP close-up).
**Longest shot:** H3–H4 (3.5 seconds — ambient breathe hold).

### Colour Grading Direction

| Aspect | Specification |
|--------|---------------|
| Overall look | Dark, cinematic, high contrast. The frame is predominantly black/very dark with the K1 light as the hero element. |
| K1 light | **Do not colour-correct the K1's actual LED output.** The colour captured on camera IS the product's colour. Grade the surrounding environment to complement, but leave the K1 light authentic. |
| Skin tones | Warm, natural. The instrument close-ups should feel tactile and human. |
| Instrument wood | Rich, warm. Emphasise the grain and texture of the drum skin and cello body. |
| Black levels | Crushed slightly — the shadows should be deep and inky, not milky. But preserve detail in the K1's lowest brightness levels. |
| Colour space | Rec. 709 for web delivery. Grade in DaVinci Resolve or equivalent. S-Log3 to Rec. 709 with a film-look LUT as a starting point, then refine. |
| Consistency | The K1's light is the colour constant across all segments. Everything else (backgrounds, instruments, skin) should feel like it belongs in the same world. |

### Audio Mix Specification

| Element | Level (relative) | Pan | Processing |
|---------|-------------------|-----|------------|
| Music stems | 0 dB (reference) | Stereo centre (drums), slight width on cello, centre on voice | Minimal. Warmth. No aggressive compression. The dynamics are essential — the build must feel like a build, not a wall. |
| Voiceover | +3 dB above music | Centre | De-ess. Gentle compression (2:1, slow attack). Slight low-cut at 80Hz. Warm plate reverb at -18dB (just enough to place the voice in a space, not enough to hear it as reverb). |
| Room tone / drone | -20 dB below music | Stereo wide | Subtle. Felt, not heard. Fills the silence without calling attention to itself. |
| Silence (0:45–0:48) | Room tone only at -30 dB | — | The silence must be real silence, not digital void. A hint of room tone prevents the audio from feeling "broken." |

**Dynamic range:** Preserve it. The peak at 0:43 should be 12–15 dB louder than the opening drone. The silence drop is the loudest moment in the video (paradoxically — the absence of sound after the peak is what hits hardest).

**Loudness target:** -14 LUFS integrated (YouTube standard). Peak at -1 dBTP.

### Title Card / End Card Design

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Wordmark | SpectraSynq brand typeface. White on black. No glow, no effects, no animation beyond a simple fade-in (0.5s). |
| "K1" | Below or beside the SpectraSynq wordmark. Same typeface, slightly smaller weight or size. |
| Layout | Centred. Generous negative space. The wordmark should feel like it is floating in darkness, just as the K1 was. |
| Duration | 2 seconds visible (0:53–0:55). Fade to full black at 0:55. |
| NO | No URL. No "pre-order now." No social handles. No QR code. This is a brand film, not a direct-response ad. CTAs live in the video description and surrounding content. |

### Delivery Formats

| Format | Aspect | Resolution | Codec | Bitrate | Use |
|--------|--------|------------|-------|---------|-----|
| Master | 16:9 | 3840x2160 | ProRes 422 HQ | ~220 Mbps | Archive and future re-edits |
| YouTube | 16:9 | 3840x2160 | H.265 (HEVC) | 40 Mbps | Primary distribution |
| YouTube (compat) | 16:9 | 1920x1080 | H.264 | 20 Mbps | Fallback for older devices |
| Instagram Reels / TikTok | 9:16 | 1080x1920 | H.264 | 15 Mbps | Social vertical |
| Instagram Feed | 1:1 | 1080x1080 | H.264 | 12 Mbps | Social square |
| Twitter/X | 16:9 | 1920x1080 | H.264 | 10 Mbps | Social landscape |

#### 9:16 Reframe Strategy

The 16:9 master is the source of truth. The 9:16 version is NOT a separate edit — it is a reframe:

- **K1 close-ups** (majority of the video): crop to centre. The K1 is horizontal; in 9:16 it sits in the middle third of the frame with black above and below. This is aesthetically appropriate — the darkness is part of the design.
- **Instrument close-ups**: crop to the action point (stick/bow/mouth).
- **Wide environmental shot (G3)**: this shot may not work in 9:16. Replace with a tighter framing or the top-down shot (G1) which reframes well.
- **Orbit shots**: track the K1 centre as the crop anchor point.
- **Wordmark**: re-layout for vertical — SpectraSynq above, K1 below, both centred.

#### 1:1 Reframe Strategy

- Centre-crop from 16:9. Most compositions are centrally weighted and survive this crop.
- The K1 horizontal bar in a square frame creates a natural "horizon line" composition.

## Quality Gates — Verification Protocol

### Gate 1: Cause-Effect Anchor

**Test:** Show segments C, D, and E to 5 people without context. Ask: "What is the relationship between the two things you see in each clip?"

**Pass criteria:** 4/5 immediately identify that the sound produces the light.

**Fail action:** Re-examine the timing between instrument shots and K1 shots. The cut must land within 1 frame of the transient. If using stock instrument footage, the visual sync may be off — re-cut or reshoot.

### Gate 2: Instrument Intelligence

**Test:** Show segments C, D, and E sequentially to 3 people without context. Ask: "Did you notice anything different about how the light behaved across the three clips?"

**Pass criteria:** 3/3 identify that different instruments produce different light behaviour. They do not need to name the specific differences — they need to notice that differences exist.

**Fail action:** The K1 effects for each instrument are not sufficiently differentiated. Options: (a) tune the effect parameters to increase visual contrast between segments, (b) adjust colour palettes to be more distinct, (c) re-examine the audio stems — if the cello and voice have similar spectral content, the K1 responses may be too similar.

### Gate 3: Goosebumps Test

**Test:** Show the complete video to 5 people in a quiet, dark room with good speakers or headphones. Observe their physical response at 0:43–0:48 (the silence drop).

**Pass criteria:** At least 2/5 exhibit a visible physical response (goosebumps, sharp intake of breath, widened eyes, lean forward, or verbal exclamation).

**Fail action:** The build (0:35–0:43) is not intense enough, OR the silence is not sudden enough, OR the preceding 40 seconds have not earned the emotional payoff. Review the audio mix dynamics (is the peak-to-silence contrast at least 15 dB?), the visual intensity at peak, and the edit pacing in the escalation segment.
