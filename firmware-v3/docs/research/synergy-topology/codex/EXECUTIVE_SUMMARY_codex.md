# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

## 1. Headline thesis

K1's strategic moat is not the GIVEN software stack by itself; it is the compounding of soft compositing, musical phase, and dual-strip centre-origin LGP geometry, filtered hard enough that the result stays liquid light instead of becoming an analyser.

## 2. Top-3 hubs identified

1. [high] D04 LayerStack Overlap Composition
   - Why: highest graph fan-out and largest numeric surface gain; one ~150 LOC compositor turns current modes into a combinatorial layer alphabet.
   - Constraint: unsafe without render-cost gating and product restraint.

2. [high] D03 Global Framebuffer LPF / Softness Pass
   - Why: converts hard-pixel effects into liquid temporal behaviour, enables Liquid Stillness, cross-fades and shared softness.
   - Constraint: must be dt-correct, opt-out capable and built on reusable persistence helpers to avoid double-trails.

3. [medium-high] D09 Dual-Strip Phase / Interference / Parallax
   - Why: the strongest physical moat; several edges are impossible on single-strip hardware.
   - Constraint: hardware A/B must prove that LGP diffusion preserves depth/interference rather than blending it into generic blur.

Honourable mentions: D05 ControlBus render-side reuse and D06 tempo phase exposure are high-leverage, but they become most valuable when attached to D04 and D09.

## 3. Recommended kill order

Phase 0: Baseline Guardrails - "K1 remains K1 while the graph expands"
  Product filter plus centre-origin audit before new capability work.

Phase 1: Soft Composition Kernel - "K1 becomes a composable liquid renderer"
  PersistenceHelpers -> Global framebuffer LPF -> LayerStack -> render-side ControlBus reuse.

Phase 2: Polished Music Geometry - "K1 becomes visibly musical, not just reactive"
  Motion-blur dot cache -> single tempoPhase01 exposure -> pitch/chroma spatialisation.

Phase 3: Dual-Strip Moat - "K1 becomes physically non-copyable on a single strip"
  Dual-strip phase/interference primitives -> Reflective Twin enforcement -> Cross-Strip Wave Interference.

Phase 4: Ambient State and Product Ritual - "K1 becomes extraordinary before and after music"
  Long-context/silence state -> First-Light Ignition -> minimal voice/music or silence classifiers.

Phase 5: Frame-History Time Depth - "K1 remembers visually"
  PSRAM frame ring -> Echo Composer/delayed Reflective Twin -> optical-flow overlay.

Phase 6: Dynamical Motion Research - "K1 simulates liquid and force"
  Heat field first, then SpringLatticeBloom or shallow-water after hardware A/B.

## 4. Top-3 surprises vs the GIVEN

1. The GIVEN misses the physical moat.
   LayerStack x LPF x ControlBus is a powerful software platform, but D09 dual-strip phase/interference is the strongest defensibility layer because SB/ES/WLED-class single-strip devices cannot reproduce it.

2. Product restraint is a graph node, not just taste.
   D15 changes the topology by killing high-surface but wrong branches: literal ES tempo-bank dots, neural tensor visualisers, scope modes and kaleidoscope-style maximalism.

3. `fx_dots[]`/sub-pixel dot cache is a small bridge with outsized value.
   It links tempo pendulum, pitch constellations and chromagram dots; without it, musically intelligent modes risk looking jittery and cheap.

## 5. Open questions Captain must resolve

1. Tempo scope: ship single `tempoPhase01` first, or commit to an ES-style per-bin tempo bank?
2. Global LPF integration: mandatory renderer pass with opt-out, or explicit opt-in per effect?
3. Dual-strip budget: how aggressively should V1.0 prioritise Cross-Strip Wave Interference and phase parallax?
4. Liquid Stillness arbitration: who chooses the 8-12 ambient programmes that survive curation?
5. Centre-origin exceptions: are circular/drift-origin ideas permanently banned, or can they exist as explicitly ratified minority modes?
6. Hardware proof threshold: what A/B evidence is required before D09 or D11 becomes launch-track?

## 6. Predicted divergence points vs the other LLM run

1. I may rank D09 higher than the other run.
   Reason: the sources repeatedly state dual-strip is under-exploited and single-strip-impossible; I treat it as near-platform, not just an effect family.

2. I demoted D03 x D04 from combinatorial to multiplicative under adversarial review.
   Reason: LPF makes layers better, but it only becomes combinatorial if softness is per-layer/state-aware. This does not change the recommended ordering.

3. I place D08 PersistenceHelpers before the GIVEN.
   Reason: without shared dt-correct persistence primitives, global LPF risks becoming a one-off and physics work later will duplicate unstable math.

4. I keep full ES tempo bank out of V1.0.
   Reason: ProductStrategy rejects literal tempo-bank swarms; single tempo phase unlocks the launch-relevant pendulum and beat-gated bloom with less risk.

5. I treat First-Light as a strategic phase, not a side effect.
   Reason: low engineering effort plus launch-video/product-ritual value makes it disproportionately important even though it is not a graph hub.
