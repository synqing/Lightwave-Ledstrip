# PASS 4 - ADVERSARIAL STRESS TEST

## ASSUMPTION REGISTRY

[A01] Assumption: The current visible-mode baseline can be modelled as 106 modes/programmes.
      Evidence: ProductStrategy references 106 ambient programmes; codebase map confirms a larger 350+ implementation surface.
      Falsification condition: production rotation has materially fewer/more eligible modes after experimental filtering.
      Collapse impact: Pass 3 headline integers scale, but ordering does not collapse because relative layer/depth/axis deltas remain.

[A02] Assumption: LayerStack can render 2-3 cheap effects under the 2.0 ms ceiling.
      Evidence: Composition SSA budgets 0.5-0.6 ms per cheap layer and flags pre-vetting; brainstorm estimates LayerStack at ~150 LOC unlocking 12 categories.
      Falsification condition: real K1 profiling shows common launch effects exceed budget when stacked.
      Collapse impact: D04 hub demotes from immediate platform to gated capability; Phase 1 must add a render-cost registry before LayerStack is usable.

[A03] Assumption: Global framebuffer LPF improves more effects than it damages.
      Evidence: ES used `apply_image_lpf` as universal trail; K1 lacks a global equivalent; brainstorm identifies it as K1's most underserved gap.
      Falsification condition: existing self-trailing effects double-trail or lose attack when LPF is applied.
      Collapse impact: D03 remains useful but must become per-effect opt-in, reducing D03 -> D04 and D03 -> D14 strength.

[A04] Assumption: Single `tempoPhase01` is enough for V1.0.
      Evidence: brainstorm recommends single-tempo first because literal bank renderings are rejected; ProductStrategy needs one convincing pendulum more than N-bin swarm.
      Falsification condition: ESV11 phase is too unstable or not exposed with sufficient confidence to hold through quiet passages.
      Collapse impact: Phase 2 slips; D06 -> D09 and D06 -> D02 weaken; F2 Centre-Phase Pendulum becomes research.

[A05] Assumption: Dual-strip LGP effects are visually legible in real hardware.
      Evidence: K1Specific and brainstorm argue two emitters illuminating one optical medium create parallax/interference moats.
      Falsification condition: LGP diffusion blends strips so strongly that phase/parallax reads as blur, not depth.
      Collapse impact: D09 remains structural for Reflective Twin but loses hero-mode status; Cross-Strip Wave Interference moves behind Liquid Bloom and Pitch Constellations.

[A06] Assumption: Centre-origin can be enforced across all meaningful effects.
      Evidence: AGENTS/CLAUDE constraints and taxonomy K1 inheritance state centre-origin as mandatory.
      Falsification condition: some valuable geometry proposals require edge or moving origins and Captain allows exceptions.
      Collapse impact: D01 as hard prerequisite weakens; graph opens asymmetric branches, but this would violate current session constraints.

[A07] Assumption: ProductStrategy filter should dominate launch sequencing.
      Evidence: source explicitly rejects literal tempo-bank dots, neural visualiser, scope modes and kaleidoscope; K1 identity is liquid centre-origin chiaroscuro.
      Falsification condition: Captain prioritises engineering novelty or diagnostic/productivity users over premium visual restraint.
      Collapse impact: D15 moves from root to late filter; greedy order becomes more defensible.

[A08] Assumption: Render-side ControlBus reuse is low risk.
      Evidence: AudioDriver marks #4, #5, #6, #7, #11 pure render-side; ControlBus already publishes substantial fields.
      Falsification condition: actual field cadence, semantics or range are not stable enough for visual drivers.
      Collapse impact: D05 loses immediate leverage; validation harness becomes Phase 1 prerequisite.

[A09] Assumption: PSRAM frame history is safe enough for V1.1.
      Evidence: K1Specific estimates 10 s frame ring at ~1.15 MB and echo rings in kB.
      Falsification condition: PSRAM bandwidth/contention or allocation policy causes render spikes or fragmentation risk.
      Collapse impact: D10 stays promising but requires allocator/perf work before product use.

[A10] Assumption: Dynamical physics will look better than refined kinematic bloom on LGP.
      Evidence: Physics SSA argues K1 unlocks state-evolving motion; K1Specific heat/shallow-water fields are cheap and LGP-native.
      Falsification condition: hardware A/B shows fields look like slow blur or simulations rather than premium liquid light.
      Collapse impact: D11 remains V1.2 research; Phase 6 should not be pulled into launch.

## MISSING DOMAINS

[M01] Signal-quality / confidence failure domain - why it matters: bad tempo, clipping, silence, speech and adversarial audio can make phase and classifier-driven effects wrong. Where it connects: [D06, D13, D14, D15].

[M02] Render-cost registry and composition scheduler - why it matters: LayerStack is only multiplicative if it can refuse unsafe layer combinations under 2.0 ms. Where it connects: [D04, D10, D11].

[M03] LGP optical transfer and diffuser variance - why it matters: dual-strip interference and heat fields may depend on diffusion strength, panel assembly and viewing angle. Where it connects: [D03, D09, D11, D18].

[M04] User-perceptual evaluation domain - why it matters: "musically meaningful" modes such as Pitch Constellations may be intellectually correct but not viscerally legible. Where it connects: [D12, D13, D15].

[M05] Power/thermal brightness budget - why it matters: LayerStack, LPF and frame-history echo can accumulate brightness; flux-conserving persistence was proposed but not elevated. Where it connects: [D03, D04, D08, D18].

[M06] Authoring and lint grammar - why it matters: without tooling, future effects can violate no-rainbow, centre-origin, Reflective Twin or layer role discipline. Where it connects: [D14, D15, D16].

## SYNERGY MIRAGE CHALLENGES

[Challenge 1] Edge [D04 x D05] claimed as combinatorial
     Counter-argument: if ControlBus fields are merely scalar knobs for layers, then LayerStack + ControlBus is just additive: more modulation, not new space.
     Defence: Composition SSA categories use audio fields to select branch, mask, layer count, parameter cross-wire and confidence, not just amplitude. This changes the alphabet of relationships among effects.
     Verdict: sustained as combinatorial, but only if LayerStack supports layer roles/masks and not just alpha blending.

[Challenge 2] Edge [D03 x D04] claimed as combinatorial
     Counter-argument: LPF plus layers may only make stacks smoother; it does not create new mode classes.
     Defence: ES global LPF made modes rely on the renderer for trails; with LayerStack it enables ambient/reactive overlay and cross-fade continuity. Still, the edge is probably multiplicative rather than truly combinatorial unless LPF parameters are per-layer or state-aware.
     Verdict: demoted from combinatorial to multiplicative for strict graph math. The kill order is unaffected because D03 still needs to precede broad D04 use.

[Challenge 3] Edge [D06 x D09] claimed as combinatorial
     Counter-argument: phase plus dual-strip can be a simple two-channel blink, not a new possibility space.
     Defence: when phase is continuous and strip roles are independent, rational ratios, beat-phase interference, strip lag and polyrhythm become a family impossible on single-strip hardware.
     Verdict: sustained as combinatorial, with a confidence gate: if tempo phase is unstable, demote to additive.

[Challenge 4] Edge [D09 x D10] claimed as combinatorial
     Counter-argument: delayed strip B is just echo.
     Defence: echo on one strip is additive; echo across two physical emitters in one LGP changes perceived depth and object unity. It is geometry plus time, not only time.
     Verdict: sustained for K1 hardware, demoted to multiplicative on any non-LGP dual-strip device.

[Challenge 5] Edge [D11 x D15] claimed as multiplicative
     Counter-argument: Product filter constrains physics; it does not amplify it.
     Defence: negative constraints increase strategic value by selecting only physics that reinforces liquid centre-origin identity. That is not mathematical multiplication, but it is topology-relevant selection.
     Verdict: sustained as "constraint edge", not counted as a numeric unlock edge in future automated sorting.

       Restructuring impact: higher compute makes full tempo banks, larger physics fields, multi-layer composition and ML formants cheaper. D11 and D13 move earlier; 2.0 ms S3 render ceiling becomes less dominant.

       Restructuring impact: D04 LayerStack, D15 lint grammar and D14 story arcs become primary; one-off hero modes become examples rather than the core product.

       Restructuring impact: D09 moat may strengthen or collapse depending on optical mixing. Any change to physical geometry forces re-validation of centre-origin, interference, parallax and Reflective Twin assumptions.

       Restructuring impact: D12 and D13 become hubs; Pitch Constellations and long-context harmonic story arcs move from V1.1/V1.2 to launch differentiators.

## CHALLENGE THE GIVEN

GIVEN claim: Global framebuffer LPF x LayerStack x ControlBus reuse is multiplicative.

Stress result:
  LPF alone is valuable but not necessarily a hub unless integrated as renderer-level, opt-out and dt-correct. If implemented as a per-effect helper only, it is additive.
  LayerStack alone is the largest numeric unlock, but without a cost registry and product filter it can create mud or brand-wrong combinations.
  ControlBus reuse alone is cheap but shallow: fields become more knobs, not new structures.

Verdict:
  The GIVEN survives, but with two corrections:
  1. The true Layer 0 should be `PersistenceHelpers -> Global LPF -> LayerStack -> ControlBus reuse`, because D08 reduces the chance that LPF becomes a one-off and provides the shared math for later physics.
  2. The GIVEN is incomplete without D15 as a guardrail. D15 is not part of the mathematical synergy chain, but it is part of the strategic topology because it changes which high-surface combinations are worth building.

Ordering challenge:
  If speed-to-demo is the only objective, Liquid Bloom tuning plus First-Light plus Cross-Strip Interference beats the GIVEN. If durable platform compounding is the objective, the GIVEN remains first.

Final adversarial judgement:
  The biggest possible mirage is not LPF or LayerStack individually. It is assuming "more combinations" means "better K1". The graph only becomes strategically correct when every new combination is forced through dual-strip centre-origin geometry and the product restraint filter.
